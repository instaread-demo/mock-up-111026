How Generation AI is reshaping consumer behaviour and commerce

Inside the new playbook for winning over "Generation AI."
By Mashable Team 
 Nov. 6, 2026
> Tech

The next era of consumer behavior is moving away from traditional search and toward AI-driven recommendations.

At Omnicom Media’s OM Forward Malaysia – Beyond Signals conference, industry leaders discussed how brands must adapt to reach what is being called "Generation AI."

Here are the key takeaways:

1. A shift in mindset, not just demographics

Generation AI isn't defined by age, but more so by a shift in behavior as consumers expect technology to anticipate and simplify their decisions.

OMD Malaysia CEO Anwer Khan noted that these consumers increasingly expect "answers over searches, recommendations over research, and personalized experiences by default."

2. From discoverability to 'recommendability'

For two decades, digital marketing focused on search engine visibility. Now, AI platforms are narrowing choices and summarizing options directly for the user.

MBCS Malaysia CEO Stanley Clement explained, "We're moving from an era of discoverability to an era of recommendability."

Brands must now evaluate how they appear in AI-powered decisions, not just search rankings.

3. The emergence of agentic commerce

Commerce is evolving to include AI as an active participant in the purchasing journey.

Flywheel Malaysia’s Head of eCommerce, Kshitij Pradeep, highlighted the move toward agentic commerce, where AI systems research, shortlist, and potentially purchase on behalf of consumers.

To succeed, brands must ensure their digital presence is easily understood and trusted by intelligent systems.

4. The enduring need for human creativity

As AI handles the processing of data and signals, original ideas become more valuable.

Brands that stand out will be those creating distinctive, memorable experiences rather than simply generating high volumes of content.

UM Malaysia CEO Sue-Anne Lim summarized this balance: "AI can help us process signals. It can't tell us what matters. That's still a human responsibility."

The transition to AI-integrated platforms mirrors past shifts toward social media and mobile. Moving forward, the challenge for brands is not just maintaining visibility, but ensuring they are actively chosen by both algorithms and people.
