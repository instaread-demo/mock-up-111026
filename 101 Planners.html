Printable Valentine Placemats

Last updated: November 10, 2026 by Nicole
Listen to audio version of this article

Make free Valentine placemats for every seat at your table or every desk in the classroom, with each child’s name, activities to keep little hands busy and a love note for the grown-ups. Print them at home in a few minutes. All free, no sign-up.

Make your placematsClassroom partyFor kidsDIY crafts

Valentine’s Day placemats usually end up in one of two places: a class party with 25 kids and a box of cupcakes, or a family dinner where the kids want something to do and the grown-ups want something pretty. The maker handles both. Add your names once, give each person the kind of placemat that suits them, and print the whole matching set in one PDF.

How to make Valentine placemats

Here is the order I would do it in.

  1. Choose a starting point. “Create my whole table” mixes kids’ activity sheets and grown-up place settings in one matching set. There are also shortcuts for a classroom party set, a coloring placemat, an activity placemat, a love notes placemat, a Valentine’s dinner and a Galentine’s placemat.
  2. Pick a style: cute love bears and hearts, soft blush watercolor, elegant roses, red gingham, bold black-and-white coloring, modern minimalist, a bright Galentine’s party look or a vintage valentine. Each comes in a few color sets, or you can match your own colors.
  3. Paste your names, one per line, and choose what each person gets: coloring and drawing, easy activities, puzzles, creative prompts for older kids and teens, love notes, conversation, or an elegant name-and-note setting.
  4. Add a note for the people you want to thank. “You make our table a lot sweeter” on a paper placemat is the part people keep.
  5. Check the plate. Turn on the dinner plate guide to see what a 10 inch plate covers, then pick your paper size.
  6. Print one PDF with every placemat, the answer keys on separate pages, and matching folded place cards and a menu card if you want them.

Everything saves on your device while you work, so you can come back the night before the party and add the new kid in class without starting over.

Valentine placemats for the classroom

A class Valentine’s party is where a placemat earns its keep. It marks each child’s seat, keeps the early finishers busy while the cards get passed out, and gives the cupcakes somewhere to land.

  • Use the Classroom party set. Paste your class list and every child gets a placemat with their name on it. Leave the list empty and you get blank placemats with a name line instead.
  • Turn on “Hide the guests’ names in the word search” and the kids hunt for their classmates’ names.
  • Every word search, maze and scramble is different for each child, so nobody copies a neighbor, and the answer keys print on their own pages for you.
  • Choose the Bold coloring style if you want to save ink. It prints in black and white with thick outlines for crayons.
  • For room parents: print one test page first, then the whole class in one go, and send the PDF to the teacher in case they want to print their own copies.

Valentine placemats for kids

The trick with kids’ Valentine placemats is matching the activities to each child, so the four-year-old and the nine-year-old are both happy.

Valentine coloring placemats for preschoolers

Choose “Coloring and drawing” and you get a big teddy bear holding a heart to color, the child’s name in outline letters, a find-and-count game with hearts, love letters and lollipops, and a connect-the-dots that turns into a heart. Switch the name style to Tracing for kids who are learning their letters.

Valentine activity placemats for early readers

Early readers like a mix: an easy maze from the bear to the box of chocolates, a small word search that runs only across and down, a drawing prompt like “Decorate this Valentine cupcake” and heart balloons to write in.

Placemats for older kids and puzzle lovers

Give older kids the Puzzle lover setting: a harder word search with diagonal and backward words, a bigger maze, a word scramble and tic-tac-toe. Teens skip the coloring but will fill in a three-panel Valentine’s comic, invent a chocolate with a ridiculous filling and answer a would-you-rather out loud.

Valentine’s Day placemats for adults

  • A name in elegant script, a short love note and the menu down one side. That is the Valentine’s dinner setting, and it works for a family dinner or a table for two.
  • One conversation question per guest, in the tone that suits your table: lighthearted, funny, meaningful, nostalgic, getting to know you, or Galentine’s and friends.
  • Galentine’s placemats in the Galentine’s party style, with a would-you-rather on every plate.
  • Faith-based wording, such as “God Is Love”, if you prefer it.

For a grown-up table I would print on 11 by 17 inch paper. It is close to the size of a real placemat and leaves room for the name and the note on either side of a 10 inch plate.

Love notes placemats

Valentine’s Day is a good excuse to say out loud what you love about the people at your table. The love notes formats in the maker include heart balloons to write in, a love wheel with one prompt per slice, three little joys, a person, a place and a moment, and a “Remember this year” keepsake with space for a drawing or a small photo. You can choose simple prompts for kids, more reflective ones, family prompts or faith-based wording.

DIY Valentine placemat crafts

A printed placemat also makes a good base for a Valentine placemat craft, for a preschool class or a snowy afternoon:

  • Fingerprint hearts. Fill the heart balloons with red and pink fingerprints.
  • Doily placemat. Glue paper doilies around the edge of a coloring placemat for a lacy, old-fashioned valentine look.
  • Tissue paper hearts. Glue scrunched squares of red and pink tissue paper onto the big hearts.
  • Woven placemat. Cut slits across a printed placemat, leaving a border, and weave red and pink paper strips through it.
  • Laminate it. Laminate a finished placemat or cover it with clear contact paper so it survives the frosting and comes back next year.

How to print Valentine placemats

A standard placemat is about 12 by 18 inches, bigger than any home printer paper, so you have a few options:

  • US Letter or A4 in landscape, which is fine for kids’ activity placemats and classroom sets.
  • 11 by 17 inch (tabloid) or A3 paper, the closest size to a real placemat. Copy shops print it, and many school and office printers do too.
  • One big placemat across several home printer sheets. The maker splits an 11 by 17 inch placemat over four Letter sheets at full size, with a shaded strip to overlap and tape.

Print in landscape at Actual size (100%), and use card stock if you can.

More for Valentine’s Day

  • A Valentines word search and a Valentine’s Day crossword puzzle, if the kids finish their placemats.
  • Valentine’s Day bingo and Valentine Pictionary for the class party.
  • The Valentine’s Day countdown, to see how long you have left.
  • Setting a proper table? Our table setting diagram shows where everything goes.
  • The same maker also makes Christmas placemats, Easter placemats and Thanksgiving placemats.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find free printable Valentine placemats?

Right here. Our Valentine placemat maker is free with no sign-up: pick a style, add the names, choose activities for each person and download a PDF of the whole set.

What do you put on a Valentine’s Day placemat for kids?

Put the child’s name in big letters, a teddy bear or hearts to color, and one or two activities that match their age, such as a maze, a word search, a find-and-count game or a drawing prompt like decorating a Valentine cupcake.

How do you make Valentine placemats for a class party?

Paste your class list into the maker and choose the Classroom party set. Every child gets a placemat with their own name and their own puzzles, and the answer keys print on separate pages. Leave the list empty to print blank placemats with a name line.

Can I make paper Valentine placemats at home?

Yes. Make them in our free maker and print them on regular printer paper or card stock in landscape. For a larger placemat, print on 11 by 17 inch paper, or split one placemat across several regular sheets and tape them together.

What size are Valentine placemats?

A standard placemat is about 12 by 18 inches. Printable Valentine placemats are usually made on 11 by 17 inch paper or A3, which is close to that size, or on US Letter or A4 paper for kids’ activity placemats.

What is an easy Valentine placemat craft for preschool?

Print a coloring placemat and let children fill the hearts with fingerprints, glue paper doilies around the edge, or weave red and pink paper strips through slits cut in the sheet.

When is Valentine’s Day 2027?

Valentine’s Day 2027 is on Sunday, February 14, 2027.
