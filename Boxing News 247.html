Frank Warren Says Denzel Bentley Owes Him 40 Grand

Frank Warren says Denzel Bentley still owes him “40 grand” after leaving Queensberry for Zuffa Boxing. Warren raised the debt on talkSPORT Boxing when asked whether he was pleased to see Bentley beat Shane Mosley Jr. in his Zuffa debut. Bentley had earlier told The Ring that he asked for a purse advance after going a year without a fight.

Video Muted

Warren used the question to defend what Queensberry had done for Bentley. He pointed to getting Bentley a world title shot against Janibek Alimkhanuly, then getting him a fight for the interim WBO middleweight title and helping secure his upgrade to full champion. Warren wished Bentley luck before adding, with laughter in the studio, “If he pays me back the 40 grand he owes me.”

Bentley gave his account of the arrangement after outpointing Mosley in London. He said he had asked to box in 2025 and was available to fight. As the year passed without a bout, he went to Warren for an advance. According to Bentley, they agreed it would come out of his purse the next time he fought.

“A whole year’s gone past. Listen, this ain’t sustainable. I need an advance,” said Bentley, recalling the conversation to The Ring. “And it’s an advance. What comes out your purse the next time you fight, we agreed next fight, you pay it back.”

Bentley said he appreciated Warren’s help and that Warren had treated him well personally. His complaint was that he felt undervalued at Queensberry and wanted more opportunities to earn a purse. Asked whether he was disappointed Warren had previously made the advance public, Bentley said the timing bothered him more. It came up the day before he fought Mosley.

The advance puts Bentley’s comments about Zuffa’s schedule in context. He said that when he had no idea when he might fight again, he hesitated to book a family trip because he might have to turn down a date. He also talked about putting down a deposit on a house, the sort of commitment he said was harder to make without knowing when he would next get paid. Zuffa’s published schedule, he said, gives him a better idea of when he might box.

Warren’s account of Bentley’s time at Queensberry focused on the title opportunities he secured, even after Bentley lost to Felix Cash, Nathan Heaney and Alimkhanuly. Bentley focused on what he could earn between those opportunities. Even with a world title on his record, he said a long stretch out of the ring left him asking for money against a future purse.

Warren did not specify a currency for the “40 grand” on talkSPORT. Bentley’s earlier interview set out the repayment terms as he understood them, but he did not say whether any money had been repaid or what balance remained.
