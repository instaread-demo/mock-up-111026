Marry Me Chicken Soup: Creamy Lasagna Style

Oh boy, do I love lasagna! It’s one of my favourite dishes ever, and this marry me chicken pasta soup recipe takes everything I love about lasagna and turns it into one seriously delicious bowl of comfort food.

We’re talking juicy chicken thighs, spicy Italian sausage, sun-dried tomatoes, broken lasagna noodles and a creamy tomato broth. And because apparently that wasn’t enough, every bowl gets a generous dollop of ricotta mixed with cheese, fresh basil, parsley and lemon zest.

The lasagna noodles cook right in the same pot, so there’s no separate pot of boiling water to deal with. It’s a hearty, family-friendly dinner that takes just over an hour, with most of the cooking happening in one Dutch oven.

If you love marry me chicken, lasagna and a really good bowl of soup, I think you’re going to love this one!

At a Glance

  • Prep time: 15 minutes
  • Cook time: Approximately 60 minutes
  • Servings: 6-8 bowls
  • Cooking method: One-pot stovetop recipe
  • Main ingredients: Chicken thighs, Italian sausage, lasagna noodles, sun-dried tomatoes, spinach and cream
  • What makes it special: Two kinds of meat, pasta cooked directly in the broth and a cheesy ricotta topping
  • Perfect for: Family dinners, chilly evenings, Sunday cooking and feeding a crowd

Recipe

Marry Me Chicken Soup: Creamy Lasagna Style

Prep Time 15 minutes minutes
Cook Time 1 hour hour
Print recipe

Ingredients:  

For the Chicken

  • 1½ lbs boneless skinless chicken thighs (about 5–6 thighs)
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp oregano

For the Soup

  • 1 lb hot Italian sausage casings removed
  • 2 tbsp oil from the jar of sun-dried tomatoes
  • 1 medium yellow onion finely diced
  • ½ cup sun-dried tomatoes packed in oil drained and chopped
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 18 oz / 540 mL can diced tomatoes, with juices
  • 7 cups chicken broth plus extra as needed
  • 8-10 lasagna noodles broken into bite-sized pieces (about 8 oz / 225 g)
  • ¾ cup heavy cream
  • 3 cups fresh baby spinach
  • ½ cup freshly grated Romano or Parmesan cheese
  • ¼ cup fresh basil chopped
  • salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

For the Ricotta Cheese Topping

  • 1 cup whole milk ricotta cheese
  • ½ cup shredded old white cheddar cheese
  • ½ cup freshly grated Romano or Parmesan cheese
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp fresh basil finely chopped
  • zest of 1 lemon
  • Pinch of salt and black pepper

Instructions: 

  • Pat the chicken thighs dry with paper towels. Season both sides with the kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, smoked paprika and oregano. Set aside while preparing the remaining ingredients.
  • Heat the sun-dried tomato oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat.
  • Add the hot Italian sausage, breaking it into small pieces with a wooden spoon. Cook for 5–7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until browned and cooked through.
  • Using a slotted spoon, transfer the sausage to a plate and set aside, leaving the cooking juices in the pot.
  • Add the seasoned chicken thighs to the same Dutch oven.
  • Sear for approximately 5 minutes per side, until golden brown. The chicken will finish cooking in the soup.
  • Remove the chicken and set aside. Once cool enough to handle, cut into bite-sized pieces. Reserve a few pieces for garnishing, if desired.
  • Reduce the heat to medium.
  • Add the diced onion to the same pot and sauté for 3–4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened.
  • Stir in the chopped sun-dried tomatoes and cook for 1 minute.
  • Add the tomato paste and cook for another 1–2 minutes, stirring to deepen its flavour.
  • Stir in the dried oregano.
  • Add the diced tomatoes with their juices and simmer for 3–4 minutes, scraping the bottom of the Dutch oven to release any browned bits.
  • Return the chopped chicken thighs and cooked Italian sausage to the pot, reserving a few pieces of chicken for serving if desired.
  • Pour in the chicken broth and stir to combine.
  • Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce the heat and simmer uncovered for approximately 15 minutes, allowing the flavours to develop.
  • Check that the chicken has reached an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C).
  • While the soup simmers, combine the ricotta, shredded old white cheddar, grated Romano or Parmesan, parsley, basil and lemon zest in a medium bowl.
  • Season lightly with salt and black pepper, then stir until well combined.
  • Cover and refrigerate until ready to serve.
  • Add the broken lasagna noodles directly to the simmering soup.
  • Stir well to prevent the noodles from sticking together.
  • Cook for approximately 10–12 minutes, or according to the package instructions, until the pasta is tender.
  • Add a splash of extra chicken broth if the soup becomes too thick.
  • Reduce the heat to low.
  • Stir in the heavy cream, followed by the fresh baby spinach.
  • Cook gently for 2–3 minutes, until the spinach has wilted.
  • Stir in the freshly grated Romano or Parmesan and chopped basil.
  • Taste and adjust the seasoning with additional salt and freshly ground black pepper, if needed.
  • Ladle the soup into individual bowls, making sure each serving has plenty of chicken, Italian sausage and lasagna noodles.
  • Top each bowl with a generous dollop of the ricotta cheese mixture.
  • Garnish with additional fresh basil, grated cheese and the reserved chicken pieces, if desired.
  • Serve immediately while hot.
  • Don’t forget to rate this recipe and leave a comment!

Enjoy!

    Why You’ll Love This Marry Me Chicken Soup

    I’ve made plenty of pasta dishes and soups over the years, but there’s something particularly satisfying about combining the two.

    It’s everything you love about lasagna. You get the noodles, tomato, cheese and Italian flavours without having to assemble all those layers. And I do love a traditional lasagna, but sometimes I want something a little different.

    The chicken and sausage combination is fantastic. I love using both because they bring different things to the pot. The chicken thighs stay juicy and tender, while the hot Italian sausage adds richness and a little heat.

    It’s a complete meal. With chicken, sausage, spinach and pasta all in one bowl, you really don’t need much else. Although I certainly wouldn’t turn down a piece of crusty bread!

    The ricotta topping makes it. Don’t skip this part. The creamy, cheesy mixture slowly melts into the hot soup as you eat, and it’s probably my favourite thing about the whole recipe.

    This is the kind of marry me soup I can see making its way into our regular recipe rotation, especially once the weather gets cold.

    The Ingredients That Make This Marry Me Chicken Soup So Good

    There are a few decisions I made with this marry me pasta recipe that I think really make a difference.

    Chicken Thighs and Italian Sausage

    I’m using boneless, skinless chicken thighs rather than breasts because they stay tender during the simmering process. They’re also full of flavour, especially after being seasoned and seared.

    The hot Italian sausage brings another layer of flavour and a little spice. I like the contrast between the two meats, and it makes the soup especially hearty.

    If you’re cooking for children or anyone who prefers less heat, mild Italian sausage is an easy substitution.

    Sun-Dried Tomatoes

    Sun-dried tomatoes are one of those ingredients that can completely change a dish.

    They have a concentrated, slightly sweet tomato flavour that works beautifully with the cream and chicken broth.

    I also use 2 tbsp of the oil from the jar to brown the sausage. Why waste all that delicious flavour? It gives us a great starting point for the soup.

    If you don’t have enough oil in your jar, you can use a little olive oil for cooking instead.

    Lasagna Noodles

    Rather than using small pasta shapes, I’m breaking 8-10 lasagna noodles into bite-sized pieces.

    I love how they look in the finished soup, and there’s something about those wide, slightly ruffled pieces of pasta that makes every spoonful feel like lasagna.

    The noodles cook directly in the broth, absorbing all that wonderful flavour as they soften.

    Just remember that pasta continues absorbing liquid as it sits, so you may need to add extra chicken broth later.

    Cream, Spinach and Fresh Basil

    The heavy cream is what gives this marry me chicken lasagna soup its lovely, silky texture.

    I’m using ¾ cup, which is enough to make the broth rich without turning it into a thick pasta sauce.

    The baby spinach adds freshness and colour, while chopped basil brings that familiar Italian flavour I love.

    Both go in toward the end so they don’t get overcooked.

    Marry Me Chicken Soup: Let’s Talk About That Ricotta Topping

    If there’s one thing I really want you to make for this marry me pasta recipe, it’s the ricotta topping.

    I’m combining whole milk ricotta with old white cheddar, Romano or Parmesan, fresh parsley, basil and lemon zest.

    I love the sharpness of the cheddar with the mild ricotta, and that little bit of lemon zest brightens everything up.

    Add a generous spoonful to each bowl just before serving. As it warms, it becomes creamy and soft, almost like the cheesy centre of a really good lasagna.

    You can also make the mixture earlier in the day and keep it refrigerated until dinner.

    If you’ve ever thought about making your own ricotta, this would be a fun recipe to try it with!

    You can make a fresh, creamy ricotta-style cheese at home by heating milk with a little cream and salt, then adding lemon juice or vinegar to separate the curds. Once strained, you’re left with soft, fresh cheese.

    I came across this helpful homemade ricotta recipe from Delish, which walks through the process. It’s much simpler than you might think!

    Store-bought ricotta works perfectly well here, but homemade would make that cheesy topping feel a little extra special.

    Tips for Making the Most Delicious Marry Me Chicken Pasta Soup Recipe

    Brown the sausage properly. Give it time to develop some colour before removing it from the pot. Those browned bits left behind add so much flavour.

    Don’t overcrowd the chicken. You want a good golden sear, not steamed chicken. Work in batches if your Dutch oven is on the smaller side.

    Sauté the tomato paste. Cooking it for a minute or two before adding the broth helps deepen the tomato flavour and takes away some of its raw acidity.

    Break the noodles into similar-sized pieces. They don’t have to be perfect, but keeping them reasonably consistent helps everything cook evenly.

    Stir the pasta occasionally. Lasagna noodles can stick together, especially when cooking in a thick soup. Give the pot a good stir as they soften.

    Keep extra broth nearby. This soup thickens as the noodles absorb liquid. A splash of warm broth will bring it back to your preferred consistency.

    Add the cream at the end. I always reduce the heat before stirring in the cream. A gentle simmer is all you need to finish the soup.

    Taste before adding more salt. Between the sausage, chicken broth and Romano cheese, there’s already plenty of seasoning in the pot.

    Can You Make Marry Me Chicken Soup Ahead of Time?

    Absolutely, although I would make one small adjustment if you’re preparing it in advance.

    You can cook the chicken, sausage and tomato broth earlier in the day, then refrigerate everything once cooled.

    When you’re ready to serve, bring the soup back to a gentle simmer and add the broken lasagna noodles.

    This way, the pasta cooks fresh and won’t become too soft while sitting in the broth.

    The ricotta mixture can also be prepared ahead and stored in a covered container in the refrigerator.

    For a dinner party, I’d get the soup base and ricotta ready beforehand, then finish cooking the noodles, cream and spinach shortly before everyone sits down.

    Marry Me Pasta Recipe: Storage and Reheating

    In the refrigerator: Store leftover soup in an airtight container for up to 3–4 days. Keep the ricotta topping in a separate container.

    Reheating: Warm the soup gently in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Add extra chicken broth as needed because the lasagna noodles will continue absorbing liquid.

    Can you freeze it? I’d recommend freezing the soup base before adding the noodles, cream and spinach. Creamy soups can separate after thawing, and cooked lasagna noodles tend to become very soft.

    For the best texture: Add freshly cooked noodles and finish with cream, spinach and ricotta when you’re ready to serve.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is Marry Me Chicken Soup?

    Marry Me Chicken Soup is inspired by the popular creamy chicken dish made with sun-dried tomatoes, herbs and cream. My version takes things a little further by adding Italian sausage, lasagna noodles and a cheesy ricotta topping.

    Can I use chicken breasts instead of thighs?

    Yes, but I prefer thighs because they stay juicier during cooking. If using chicken breasts, be careful not to overcook them.

    Can I use a different pasta?

    Definitely! If you love a traditional marry me pasta recipe, you can use smaller pasta shapes instead of lasagna noodles. Just adjust the cooking time according to the package directions and add more broth if necessary.

    Is this Marry Me Pasta Soup spicy?

    The hot Italian sausage adds some heat, but the cream and ricotta help balance it. For a milder version, use mild Italian sausage.

    Why did my soup become so thick?

    The lasagna noodles absorb broth as they cook and continue absorbing it after the soup is finished. Simply stir in a little extra warm chicken broth until you reach the consistency you like.

    A Little Bit of Everything I Love

    I have a serious weakness for lasagna, and this soup gives me all those familiar flavours in a completely different way.

    There’s the richness of the sausage, the tender chicken, the creamy tomato broth and those big pieces of lasagna noodles. And then there’s the ricotta, which I would happily put on just about everything!

    I also love that it’s a meal the whole family can enjoy without having to prepare a bunch of separate dishes.

    Whether you’re making it for a busy weeknight, a relaxed Sunday dinner or even feeding a houseful of family around Christmas, this is one recipe I think you’ll come back to.

    And if you’re anything like me, you’ll be looking forward to the leftovers before you’ve even finished your first bowl!
