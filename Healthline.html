2026 Healthline Women’s Health Survey Findings: Uncovering the Barriers to Care

Medically reviewed by Stacy A. Henigsman, DO — Written by Anisha Mansuri on November 3, 2026
  • Barriers to care
  • Dismissal across life stages
  • Conclusion

Key Insights

  • Women report a range of challenges when seeking healthcare support, with 31% reporting negative experiences with healthcare professionals, 23% feeling their concerns were dismissed, and 41% having physical symptoms attributed to emotional causes.
  • Three out of 5 women had to see 2 or more doctors before receiving a clear diagnosis or treatment. Those with endometriosis, postpartum depression, and PMOS reported the highest rates of dismissal.
  • Experiences of dismissal occurred across women’s life spans, from menstruation through menopause, with 65% of respondents feeling they always need to advocate for their needs at the doctor’s office.

For women navigating the healthcare system, managing symptoms can be only part of the challenge. Feeling heard, having their concerns taken seriously by healthcare professionals (HCPs), and finding the time to seek care can all create further obstacles.

The 2026 Healthline Women’s Health Survey explored these challenges to better understand the disparities that impede women’s access to quality healthcare and the realities of seeking support.

The survey was conducted by Fullspan Health, a B2B healthcare platform under Healthline’s parent company, RVO Health. We surveyed 1,032 women to deepen our understanding of the barriers they face in accessing effective healthcare. Here are the key findings.

About the survey

  • Survey details:
    • Sample size: 1,032 people
    • Fielding dates: May 1 to May 28, 2026
  • Respondents:
    • Age range: Women age 25 and older
    • Caregiver status: 474 women who make health and wellness decisions for loved ones
    • Employment status: 499 women in employment
      • 242 respondents said they had to take unpaid time off
      • 257 respondents said they did not have to take unpaid time off
  • Survey approach:
    • Survey type: On-demand consumer survey conducted digitally
    • Response format: Quantitative questions and open-ended feedback

Being heard remains a major barrier for women when accessing care

For many women, systemic obstacles such as dismissal can influence how they navigate healthcare and the quality of care they receive. The Healthline survey found that while most women reported feeling heard by HCPs, 31% said they had a negative or problematic experience.

Among the women we surveyed, the most significant challenge was not feeling heard by HCPs when seeking care. When respondents were asked to describe a personal experience with how well their doctors understand women’s health needs, 64% reported a positive experience, with 15% noting a positive experience with a female doctor.

However, 23% felt their concerns were dismissed, and 8% experienced bias, discrimination, or rudeness from a HCP. Another common experience was physical symptoms being dismissed as emotional, with 41% of women saying this happened some of the time or more often.

For many women, finding answers about their health concerns can involve navigating multiple appointments. The survey found that 3 out of 5 women had to see 2 or more doctors before getting a clear diagnosis or treatment, and 65% felt as though they always need to advocate for their needs at the doctor’s office.

When I was starting peri menopause and having hot flashes, I went to the doctor several times. I kept being told I was too young and it was in my head.” — Survey respondent

Those living with conditions that uniquely affect women are most likely to report negative care experiences

The findings highlight a notable pattern across several areas of women’s health. Respondents living with endometriosis, postpartum depression, and polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) were among those who reported the highest rates of negative care experiences.

Gaps in research and clinical understanding of women’s health may be an important consideration when examining the barriers reported by those surveyed. In the Healthline survey, 14% of respondents felt their HCP lacked knowledge of women’s health, 23% feared not being understood, and 20% reported that a HCP misdiagnosed their symptoms.

One time, a doctor wouldn’t tell me what was wrong and blamed it on my period; later, I needed to go back in for major pain. Turns out the whole time I had endometriosis and needed surgery to solve the issue.” — Survey respondent

Women report experiences of dismissal across the female life span

Women’s experiences of feeling dismissed are not confined to a single health condition but can occur throughout their life span.

The survey highlights a recurring theme across several areas of women’s health, with 39% of respondents experiencing perimenopause or menopause reporting dismissal by HCPs. The same proportion was reported by respondents with fertility issues, rising to 47% among those with endometriosis and 46% among those with postpartum depression.

While these conditions and experiences are distinct and won’t be part of every woman’s health journey, the findings suggest that dismissal can have wider implications for individual healthcare interactions: almost a quarter of respondents said they feared being misunderstood when seeking care, and 19% were concerned about being perceived as difficult.

Experiences of anticipated and repeated dismissal may also affect women’s trust in healthcare services and their confidence in raising health concerns, with 6% of respondents saying they avoided seeking care or did not trust doctors. Having to spend considerable time and energy justifying symptoms and pushing for concerns to be taken seriously may also diminish expectations in healthcare systems and care-seeking opportunities.

Throughout the different stages of women’s lives, their healthcare needs evolve along with their bodies, from menstruation through to menopause. These findings reinforce the importance of healthcare that meets women at each of these stages and responds to their individual needs.

I feel like we are misunderstood so often especially as we deal with hormonal issues, whether it’s [postpartum] depression or perimenopause. We are already not feeling ourselves and do not understand why then we have this doctor who doesn’t listen to issues.” — Survey respondent

How experiences of dismissal can shape women’s future healthcare decisions

These findings shed light on the disparities that can impact women’s access to effective healthcare and the realities of seeking support. For many women, dismissal can create additional obstacles that go beyond recognizing symptoms and making a medical appointment.

Women may also feel the need to prepare for the possibility that their concerns may not be taken seriously and to research their symptoms in advance so they feel well-equipped to advocate for their needs.

When they do seek support, some may still face dismissal and need to push for their symptoms and concerns to be acknowledged. This may involve getting second opinions and consulting multiple doctors, which can complicate the process of seeking care and potentially delay an effective treatment plan being implemented.

Navigating these obstacles can demand considerable time and energy, often while women are still living with the symptoms that led them to seek care in the first place. Finding the time to try again can create an additional demand that some women may not have the capacity for.

The experiences raised in this survey highlight why access to healthcare involves more than being able to book an appointment. Women also need to feel confident that their concerns will be taken seriously, without feeling that securing effective care depends on how well they can advocate for themselves.

Ultimately, women should not have to prepare for potential dismissal before they even walk into a doctor’s office. Having your health concerns taken seriously should be a standard part of every woman’s healthcare experience, where being heard is not the exception but the expectation.

“Women should not have to fight to be heard or put their own health last. Yet these findings reveal the barriers many face while navigating their own care, managing their families’ health and balancing career demands. Supporting women starts with taking their concerns seriously and making care easier to navigate. At Healthline, we’re committed to turning evidence-based information into practical guidance that helps women understand their options, advocate for their needs and take the next step in their care with confidence.”

— Christine Maguire, EVP and Chief Business Officer at RVO Health
